Who it is for

Many people lose their voice suddenly, but can still move their lips.

🎗️

After a laryngectomy

Removal of the voice box, most often for throat cancer — among the most common cancers in India.

🏥

ICU patients

On a ventilator or with a tracheostomy tube: fully awake, but unable to make a sound.

🧠

Stroke & paralysis

Stroke survivors and people with vocal-cord paralysis or neurological conditions.

Today they point at alphabet boards, write on paper or gesture: slow, tiring, and impossible when the hands are weak. Families and nurses are left guessing — and pain signals get missed.

How it works

Everything runs on the laptop, in real time. No special sensors, no GPU.

  1. 1

    See

    The webcam feeds Google's MediaPipe Face Landmarker: 478 face points and 52 facial-muscle scores, every frame.

  2. 2

    Read the lips

    40 lip points become a motion signature. A deep model — five small neural networks trained on the user's own clips — recognises the phrase. Instant templates double-check it.

  3. 3

    Decide safely

    Sure? It speaks. Unsure? It shows its top 3, or asks an LLM to pick using the conversation — the LLM can never invent a phrase. Emergencies alert the caregiver.

  4. 4

    Speak

    In the user's own recorded or cloned voice, levelled to a clear loudness, with sound cues so nobody has to watch the screen.

  5. 5

    Learn

    Every correction becomes a new example, and the deep model retrains itself when the app closes.

Features

🧠

Deep learning on your own lips

An ensemble of 5 neural networks with heavy augmentation, checked by honest cross-validation — and only used if it beats the simpler method.

🗣️

Your own voice

Voice banking: each phrase recorded once in the user's voice, or cloned. AI-generated audio is always labelled as such.

😊

Emotion & pain reading

Seven expressions and a pain score from the facial muscles, using the psychologists' Facial Action Coding System.

🚨

Emergency & pain alarms

"I can't breathe" or a held pain expression sound an alarm for the caregiver that repeats until acknowledged.

💧

Picture symbols

Every phrase has a symbol, like the communication boards used in hospitals — tap any tile to speak it.

🛟

Safe when unsure

A low-confidence guess is never spoken. Repeated tries add up, and 1/2/3 picks from the top three.

💬

Context-aware

An LLM (Groq, Claude, Gemini or a local model) chooses between close guesses using the conversation and the facial expression.

🔔

Sound you can follow

Soft chimes for listening, done, unsure and learned; distinct alarms; volume keys; every clip levelled to one loudness.

🌐

Any language

Add your own phrases in any language or script, such as Hindi or Nepali, and teach them in a minute.

🔒

Private by design

Stores only lip coordinates, never video. Works fully offline; the emotion reading can be switched off.

One speaker, 58 clips of 10 phrases, recorded in two sittings, on a 2-core laptop. The built-in self-check, which trains fewer networks to save time, reported 77% vs 67%. A new day or new lighting will score lower, and more clips per phrase raise accuracy — we report what we measured, including the limits.

Reading the face, not just the lips

A patient who can't speak also can't say how they feel. SilentSpeech reads the expression from the same 52 muscle scores, using the Facial Action Coding System that psychologists use, and a pain score based on the clinical PSPI pain measure.

It learns each person's resting face, knows that mouthing words moves the lips and brows, and treats the result as a hint for the caregiver — never a diagnosis.

Responsible by design

Privacy

Only lip coordinates are stored, never video. Everything runs on the laptop; the LLM sees phrase text and a few words about the expression, never images.

Safety

Unsure guesses are never spoken. Emergency phrases use a lower alert threshold, because missing an emergency is worse than a false alarm.

Honesty

Real measured numbers, limits included. A cloned voice is labelled as AI-generated. A research prototype, not a medical device.

Current limits: a fixed set of phrases rather than free sentences; each person calibrates their own clips; tongue and throat sounds are invisible to a camera; very dim light, masks or tubes over the mouth block the lips.

Run it yourself

  1. Download the code from GitHub (Code → Download ZIP).
  2. Double-click start.bat (macOS / Linux: sh start.sh). It installs everything, checks the camera, and calibrates: mouth 10 phrases, 4 times each (about 10 minutes).
  3. Afterwards, open it with app.bat or the SilentSpeech icon on the desktop.

Needs a laptop with a webcam and Python 3.10, 3.11 or 3.12. Works offline; an LLM key is optional.
